A chart is for everyone who reads the page: with a mouse, with a keyboard, with a screen reader, without telling
colours apart, or on paper. None of this is an option to turn on: every chart does it.

Press Tab to reach the chart's points, then the arrow keys; the data table under the chart is the one screen readers
read.

## The keyboard

The chart's points are one tab stop: Tab lands on the point focused last (the first time, the first point of the first
visible series). Tab again goes on to the legend's items, then out of the chart.

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| ← → | the previous or next point along x, as drawn (a mirrored axis turns them with it) |
| ↑ ↓ | the series above or below, at the same x; in bars, along the categories |
| Home, End | the series' first and last point |
| Page Up, Page Down | a tenth of the series' points |
| Enter | a click on the point (`pointClick`) |
| Escape | hides the tooltip; the focus stays |
| `+` `-` `0` | with `zoom`: zoom in and out around the point, back to the whole |

The focused point has a visible ring (`--dzgraph-focus`), and the crosshair and tooltip of a hover. Every point can be
reached, also those a dense line draws without a marker: the ring shows where it is. The legend's items are toggle
buttons: Enter or Space hides or shows a series.

## Screen readers

The chart is a figure named by its title, described by a summary written for it: its kind, its series and the range
of each, then the app's `description` when it gives one. Each visible series is a list (« Plum, 6 points »), each
point an item read as « Plum, Aug: 66. Point 4 of 6. ». A series of more than 500 points is not written out: one item
stands for the focused point and changes as the focus moves.

Axes, gridlines, tick labels and the legend's signs are hidden from screen readers: what they say is in the summary,
the points and the data table. A polite live region says what the user changed (« Quince hidden »); data updates are
not announced. The words are English or French, after the page's language; others can be given (see
[the chart's words](../theming-rtl/#the-charts-words)).

## The data table

Every chart has a table with the same numbers: a caption (the title), a first column of x values, then one column per
visible series, values written as in the tooltip. It is hidden from sight but read by screen readers, after the chart;
`dataTable: true` shows it under the chart.

- It has at most 1 000 rows; past that, its last row says how many were left out (the [CSV
  export](../exporting/#the-numbers-as-csv) gives them all).
- Zoomed, it shows the visible range and says so in its caption; a button under it switches to all the data and back.
  `dataTable: { show: true, rows: 'all' }` starts with all the data.

## Never colour alone

Colour is never the only way to tell series apart:

- Each series has its own marker symbol: circle, square, diamond, triangle, triangle pointing down, then again. A line
  shows markers on every point when they are far enough apart, and about every 80 px when it is dense, each series
  from its own offset. `marker: true` puts one on every point.
- The legend shows each series' sign: its line with its marker, or a square.
- The theme's eight series colours were chosen for colour-blind readers (neighbours stay apart), and each stands at
  least 3:1 against the chart's background, in light and dark; text stands at 4.5:1. A test holds these contrasts.
- In forced colours (Windows high contrast), the chart takes the system's colours and keeps its symbols.

## Motion

A chart appears and changes with a short, sober animation. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` there is none: the
end state shows at once. `animation: false` turns it off for every reader.

<Aside>
The chart does its part; the page does the rest: give the chart a title, put it near the text that speaks of it, and
say in `description` what a reader should see in it.
</Aside>
